eduSync — Founder Validation

Written as a skeptical founder who has to decide whether to put the next two years into this. Reviewed: the whole edusync/ workspace (strategy, product, architecture, legal, brand, research, 15-slide deck, financial model). Date: 2026-11-10.

Verdict: the plan as written will fail. A much narrower version has a small but real chance, and you can find out which one you have in 90 days.

"As written" means: global, six revenue layers, 10 million learners, $478.7M revenue in Year 5, 60% EBITDA margin. Nothing in the folder supports that plan. The only part with a real shot is the thin slice inside it: one role (Junior Data Analyst), one city, employers paying only for a hire that is still employed after the retention period.

The sentence you can say out loud:
"The global edtech version of eduSync will not work, and I'm not going to pretend it will. What I can still test is one narrow thing: will real employers hire people who pass our graded tasks and pay us when they stay? I'll know in 90 days, and if the answer is no, I stop."

My probabilities (judgment, not data): global plan as written succeeds: under 1%. Narrow wedge gets 5 retained hires from 3 employers within ~6 months of real effort: about 15%. That wedge grows into a venture-scale company: about 2–3%. Those numbers move a lot with the first 10 employer conversations (section 9).

How to read this. CHECKED = arithmetic or fact I recomputed from your files. JUDGMENT = my opinion from how ventures like this usually go; I have no data on your market. I have not interviewed a single employer or learner for you, and neither does anything in the folder. That absence is the biggest finding below.

What's wrong with the model, in order

  1. It has no measured inputs. Placement rate, learner-to-hire conversion, cost per learner, fee per hire and sales cycle are all assumed. The pilot should replace every one with a measured number.
  2. Revenue is top-down growth, not bottom-up. There is no "N employers × H hires × fee" tied to an employer pipeline.
  3. Six revenue layers on a company with zero revenue. Each layer is a separate business (B2C, institutions, employers, placement, government, marketplace). Pick the one that pays first.
  4. Government and institution revenue have 6–18 month sales cycles. JUDGMENT They cannot be Year 2 revenue for a team of ~10.
  5. Fee on salary can be illegal or restricted. Charging jobseekers a share of salary is restricted in many places (see ILO Convention 181 principle: workers should not be charged fees). Employer-paid is the safe lane. I have only flagged this; local legal advice is required.

I could not reconcile the placement-fee revenue line to 500,000 placements without knowing the average salary assumption in the spreadsheet; ask whoever built it to show the formula chain from placements to revenue.

8. What the MVP proves, and what it cannot

The app in edusync-app/ is built to measure the loop, not to look finished. 66 automated end-to-end checks pass against the real server (grading, SQL sandbox attacks, authz, tamper detection, realtime events, fee ledger).

What the code cannot prove

  • That an employer trusts the credential. Only an employer saying "I interviewed because of it" proves that.
  • That the person who passed is the person who did the work. Paste/time signals are shown to employers but can be gamed.
  • That the live Claude tutor behaves well; I tested the integration against a mock only, and the UI has not been visually checked by me (the browser tool would not connect).
  • Retention and fee collection. The ledger records a fee only after an employer marks someone retained. Real invoicing is manual.

9. The 90-day test, with stop rules

What would upgrade my verdict

  • 3+ employers sign a written LOI to interview graduates before you build more.
  • 5 learners reach interviews because of the credential (employer says so in writing).
  • Cost per retained hire (AI + ops + acquisition) is below the fee. Compute this honestly.

What would make it a confirmed fail

  • After 10 employer conversations, fewer than 3 will even pilot. The pain is not real enough.
  • Learners pass assessments but employers decline to interview them. The credential has no value.
  • Hires happen but nobody can show eduSync caused them. Employers would have hired them anyway, so nobody pays.

10. What to say when you must tell someone it will fail

Short version (use this): "As planned, global and six revenue streams at once, it will fail. The financial model has no measured inputs and assumes a placement rate that jumps 50× with no explanation. What survives is a single test: one role, one city, employers pay only for retained hires. We will know in 90 days. If three employers will not commit by day 30, we stop."

If they push back "so it's a bad idea?": "The idea is fine. The plan is not. A good idea with an unproven loop is a hypothesis. We turned it into a test."

What not to say: "It will fail" with no conditions. It is not proven that it fails. It is proven that nothing in the folder shows it works.
